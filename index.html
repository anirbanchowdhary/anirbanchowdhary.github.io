Anirban Chowdhary

Hello visitor!

I am a Senior Research Fellow and a PhD Candidate at the School of Astrophysics in Presidency University, Kolkata. I did my undergraduate and postgraduate degrees from the Department of Physics in Presidency University.

My research lies in theoretical and computational astrophysics, at the interface of cosmology, galaxy formation and evolution. I study how supermassive black holes, galaxies and dark matter halos co-evolve. I use state-of-the-art cosmological hydrodynamic simulations such as IllustrisTNG and SIMBA to model the halo occupation distribution and conditional luminosity functions of quasars, and to understand how AGN feedback shapes where active black holes live. My goal is to bridge simulation predictions with observations from next-generation galaxy surveys by developing statistical measures of galaxy and quasar clustering that can break degeneracies between theoretical models.

Beyond research, I am actively involved in science outreach. I have been a resource person for an IAU Office for Astronomy Education teachers’ workshop and served on the organizing committee of COBRA 2024. I have a strong passion for teaching and mentoring. Currently, I am mentoring two Master’s students with their thesis. I have worked as a physics teacher at Techno India Group Public School and as a freelance tutor.

Beyond academics and research, I have a very vast area of interests and hobbies. I am mostly an outdoor person. On weekends, I like travelling, riding bikes, and exploring food joints around the city.

selected publications

  1. ApJ
    Halo Occupation Distribution of Quasars: Dependence on Luminosity, Redshift, Black Hole Mass and Feedback Modes
    Anirban Chowdhary, and Suchetana Chatterjee
    The Astrophysical Journal, 2025
    Abs DOI arXiv Bib

    We use cosmological hydrodynamic simulations (IllustrisTNG and SIMBA) to explore the redshift, luminosity, and black hole mass dependence of the quasar halo occupation distribution (HOD). In both simulations, we find that quasar activity is quenched at a characteristic halo mass scale ( 10^13 Msun), affecting the nature of its occupation distribution function.
