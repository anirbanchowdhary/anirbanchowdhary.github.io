### How I got here

I grew up in a small town without much exposure to the world of science. For a long time, I did not know that research was something a person could do for a living. What I did have was curiosity: I was always an inquisitive child, wanting to know how things worked.

It was my professors in college who turned that curiosity into a path towards astrophysics. They showed me that the questions I kept asking could become research. That same curiosity now drives my work on how black holes, galaxies and dark matter halos grow together.

### Mountains and hitchhiking

I grew up at the foothills of the Himalaya, and the mountains have stayed with me. Whenever I can, I take short trips to the villages around, usually by hitchhiking. It slows a journey down and puts you in conversation with people you would never otherwise meet. Connecting with people and their culture is the main reason I travel.

My first hitchhike, to Sandakphu, has been the most memorable. At 3,636 metres on the Singalila Ridge, Sandakphu is the highest point in West Bengal, and on a clear day you can see Kangchenjunga and Everest from the top.

### Food in Kolkata

Kolkata has become a new home and my favourite food destination. If you are visiting, this is where I would point you:

- **Apanjan** for fish fry, a Kolkata classic
- **Kafulok** for Indo-Chinese, a cuisine this city has made its own
- **Sienna Cafe** for modern Bengali fusion, when you want something new
- **Kasturi** for Bengali cuisine, when you want the traditional thing

### Teaching

Teaching has to be the most active form of learning. You can follow a derivation or a result and still not really own it. Explaining it to a student shows you exactly where your understanding runs out.

I have taught physics at school, worked as a tutor, and now mentor student research projects. Each of these has sent me back to the basics. You can read more on the [teaching & outreach]({{ '/teaching/' | relative_url }}) page.

### The gym

I also love spending time in the gym. It is a good counterweight to long hours in front of code and simulation data, and unlike research, the progress is easy to measure.
